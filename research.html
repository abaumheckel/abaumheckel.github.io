Interests and Current Research

My interests primarily lie in trying to figure out how to get students to think more critically. It seems like sometimes students take courses and at the end of the courses are left with abstract tools but without the ability to apply those tools, even within mathematics itself. One proposed way to encourage critical thinking is by using some notion of "open-ended" problems, which might then let students use their tools more effectively.
I am currently working with the POME group at UNL, consisting of Kristen Amman, Allan Donsig, Yvonne Lai, and Angela Vichitbandha, which is concerned with investigating the differences between the math taught in classes from the math department and the math required by their engineering classes as well as ways we can change instruction to help students more effectively navigate any differences we see.
I am also working on a couple of my own projects. I have been conducting interviews with mathematicians to ask them about problem selection across the contexts of their own research, mentoring undergraduate students in research, and undergraduate courses. I then started asking myself what exactly undergraduate research looks like. There is something that happens that takes an undergraduate student taking "standard" math classes and ends up creating an undergraduate student researcher. I am interested in looking at the process of this transition. To that end, I have conducted about 25 hours of informal observations and have started interviewing faculty who have taught or are teaching courses in undergraduate research or project-based courses.
